# Storytelling philosophy: the "but / therefore" rule

Source: Trey Parker and Matt Stone, on writing episodes of South Park. Transcript paraphrased from the YouTube short referenced in the prep-agent design discussion.

> Each individual scene has to work as a kind of funny sketch. You don't want one scene that's just, like, "well, what was the point of that scene?"
>
> We found out this really simple rule. We can take these beats — which are basically the beats of your outline — and if the words that belong between those beats are "and then," you're up. In between every beat that you've written down is either the word **therefore** or **but**.
>
> So what I'm saying is: you come up with an idea and it's like, okay, this happens, and then this happens. **No no** — it should be, this happens, **therefore** this happens, **but** this happens, **therefore** this happens.
>
> As soon as we are able to — and literally sometimes we'll write it out to make sure we're doing it — we'll have our beats and we'll say, "okay, this happened, **but** then this happens, and that affects this, and that leads to that" — that's why you get a show that feels like, okay, this to that to this to that, **but** here's the complication, **therefore** that.

## Applied to podcast interview prep

A good arc of interview questions follows the same rule:

- **Therefore**: the next question *builds on* the previous answer. It deepens, specifies, or draws the logical next consequence. ("Given that shift, where does X go from here?")
- **But**: the next question *complicates or pivots* the previous answer. It introduces a tension, a contrary data point, something the guest has said elsewhere, or a case the prior answer doesn't cover. ("You said X on [other show] — how do you square that with what you just told me?")

A question list connected only by "and then" — topic, topic, topic — has no narrative spine. The guest's answers feel like separate interviews stapled together. But/therefore questions build a single through-line: a state of affairs, a complication, a consequence, a sharper complication, a synthesis.

Every question in a prep doc should be classifiable as **[therefore]** or **[but]**. If a question is just "and then here's a new topic," it should be reworked until it either follows from or challenges what came before.
