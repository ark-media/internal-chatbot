## Intro

It is 10AM on Wednesday April 22nd here in New York City. It is 5PM on Wednesday April 22nd in Israel, where the limited events of Israel's Independence Day come to a close.

When wars end, they often reshape the very politics that set them in motion. So today we ask — what will be the impact of the Iran War on American politics?

My guest today is Ross Douthat — host of the podcast Interesting Times. Ross is a New York Times columnist and conservative commentator known for his sharp analysis of politics, culture, and religion. He is also the author of several books exploring faith, society, and the future of American public life.

Ross — welcome to the Call me Back podcast.

## Questions

1. Let's start with the current state of affairs. How do you assess the current state of the war?
2. For better or worse, war accelerates political outcomes. If we compare the domestic politics of Feb 27th to now, April 22nd, what do you think has already changed?
3. You were recently on Jonah Goldberg's podcast and you said "This war is a big risk from the point of view of Zionist conservatism." How do you define that risk, and how do you think it's going to play out?
4. Antisemitism on the Left is now a permanent fixture of American politics, but over the past year, we've also seen antisemitism rising on the Right. The question now is how pervasive is it and how permanent? How would you answer that question at this point in time?
5. Do you think it's a quantitative change, or a substantive change? Has it morphed into something else?
6. 90% of what Tucker Carlson says could arguably come out of Rashida Tlaib's mouth. Do you see a real political scenario in which the far Right and far Left meet on the other end and align against the Jews? I guess what I'm asking is — could they hate Israel more than they hate each other?
7. There has been an ongoing debate about the post-Trump future of the Republican Party, really since he took office in 2017. How do you calculate the Iran War into this question?
